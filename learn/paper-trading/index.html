PAPER TRADING

How the Tickrift paper trading simulator works.

A practical explanation of the virtual-money trading mode and the limits of simulated execution.

Paper trading uses virtual money. A paper order changes only the Tickrift simulation. It cannot reach a broker or move real funds.

What paper trading is

Paper trading is a way to rehearse market decisions without placing a real-money order. In Tickrift, a paper account starts with virtual cash. Opening a position changes that virtual cash, the position list and the simulated profit or loss.

The useful part is practicing a repeatable process: choosing an instrument, defining an order, planning an exit and reviewing the result. The limitation is equally important: simulated execution cannot reproduce every real-world cost or emotional pressure.

What the Trade mode models

DirectionLong and short

Long positions gain in the simulation when price rises; short positions gain when price falls.

OrdersMarket, limit and stop

Market orders fill immediately in the simulator. Limit and stop orders wait for the displayed price to reach their trigger.

Risk controlsStop-loss and take-profit

Optional exits monitor the simulated position and close it when the specified paper threshold is reached.

ReviewPositions and journal

The bottom trading dock keeps open positions visible while the chart remains on screen.

Why simulation results are not real-world results

Real markets include bid-ask spreads, liquidity, queue priority, changing fees, latency, halts and other execution details. Tickrift uses simplified slippage and fees. A strategy that appears successful in a simulation can perform differently with real money.

How to use the simulator

  • Choose an instrument and check whether its badge says MARKET, DELAYED or SIMULATED.
  • Use the chart and timeframe controls to inspect the displayed data.
  • Set the paper order size and, if useful for the exercise, a stop-loss or take-profit.
  • Keep the Trades panel open at the bottom of the terminal so the position can be closed without leaving the chart.
  • Record the reason for the decision in the Journal and review the outcome later.
